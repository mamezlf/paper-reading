Background

The Original Paper

"False Friends Are Not Foes" (Stanford, 2025) Not all overlap is equal.
  • Trained bilingual models on 6 English-X language pairs
  • Insight: semantic similarity of shared tokens matters
  • Tested: Full, High-sim, Low-sim, and No Overlap
  • Finding: High-similarity overlap helps most

JA–ZH Testbed03 / 16

Background

Why Kanji Sharing?

  • Japanese and Chinese share a large number of Kanji characters
  • Analysis of the XLM-R tokenizer reveals approximately 26,360 naturally overlapping tokens
  • Most overlaps are digits, Latin characters, and punctuation, whose meanings are largely language-independent
  • Kanji are the primary source of meaningful overlap

Research Focus08 / 16

Motivation

Hypothesis

If overlap mainly works through semantic transfer, then similarity should determine whether sharing helps.

  • High-similarity kanji sharing should improve alignment
  • Low-similarity kanji sharing may weaken alignment or introduce interference
Transfer Hypothesis09 / 16

Methodology

Similarity Partitioning

For each token t in overlap set O (kanji only, ~4,276 tokens):

  1. Sample 100 sentences from JA & ZH CCMatrix
  2. Extract XLM-R contextual embeddings → static e_ja, e_zh
  3. Compute cosine similarity between e_ja and e_zh

Layer Selection
